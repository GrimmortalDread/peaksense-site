PEAKSENSE — Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 4, 2026

This policy covers the PeakSense desktop app for Windows ("PeakSense", "the app") and the website peaksense.net. PeakSense is made by an independent developer ("we", "us"). Questions or complaints about privacy: support@peaksense.net.

The short version
  • PeakSense runs on your own PC. Your videos, recordings, transcripts, account tokens and settings stay on your computer. Sign-in tokens are stored encrypted.
  • PeakSense contacts a service (YouTube, Twitch, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook and the others listed below) only to do something you set up or asked for, such as signing in, finding your streams, or posting your clip.
  • PeakSense uses YouTube API Services. By using its YouTube features you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service, and Google's handling of your data is described in the Google Privacy Policy. See section 7.
  • PeakSense never downloads videos from YouTube.
  • No ads, no third-party content, no analytics or tracking, and we never sell or share your data.
  • Disconnecting YouTube or TikTok in PeakSense revokes its access and deletes what PeakSense got from that platform, at once. To delete everything, see Data deletion.

1. What the app handles

Depending on the features you use, PeakSense handles the following. All of it is stored and processed on your computer.

  • Your videos: OBS recordings made while PeakSense watches your stream; video files you import or drop in, or that land in folders you choose to watch (for example your YouTube Studio or Twitch downloads, ShadowPlay or ReLive recordings); VODs and clips from your own connected Twitch channel; Kick VODs you paste; and the clips, montages, recaps, thumbnails and captions made from them.
  • Analysis results: speech transcripts made by a speech-recognition model that runs on your PC, loudness and motion measurements, gameplay events detected from the picture and game sounds, optional speaker labels, and moments found during a live stream.
  • Account information from platforms you connect: for YouTube, your channel's ID, name, handle, description and profile picture; for Twitch, your name and channel ID; for TikTok, your creator nickname, avatar and posting options; for Instagram, your username and account type; for Facebook, your name and the names of the Pages you manage.
  • Sign-in tokens issued by the platforms you connect (Google/YouTube, Twitch, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, including Facebook Page tokens) and, if you add them, a Hugging Face token and a Discord or Slack webhook address.
  • Data about your own channel: your uploads and live broadcasts (titles, descriptions, tags, durations, thumbnails, dates), your playlists, your channel's subscriber, view and video counts, and the view, like and comment counts of videos you posted through PeakSense.
  • YouTube watch-time data (only if you allow "Watch time"): for your own channel and videos: views, watch time, average view duration and percentage watched, how much of each part of a video is watched or rewatched, subscribers gained and lost, and the share of your views by age group, gender and country. These are totals and percentages for your whole audience; PeakSense never receives information about individual viewers.
  • Comments on your YouTube videos: the latest comments on your channel that haven't been answered yet (the commenter's public display name, the comment text, its time and like count), so you can answer them in PeakSense.
  • Live stream information (Twitch): whether you're live, your stream title and category, and events such as raids, subscriptions, bits, channel point redemptions and chat messages, including the chatting viewer's username and message text. See section 5.
  • OBS connection details: the address, port and WebSocket password of your OBS, and the file names of its recordings.
  • What you enter: titles, descriptions, captions, hashtags, comment replies, schedules, recipes, game profiles and settings, plus your PeakSense Pro licence key.
  • Logs: a local log file of what the app did, used for troubleshooting. Sign-in tokens and secrets are removed from logs and from the diagnostics file you can export.

PeakSense does not collect advertising identifiers, does not read the cookies or data of your web browsers, and never asks for your YouTube, Google, TikTok, Twitch, Instagram or Facebook password: you sign in on each platform's own page. It sends no crash reports or usage counts unless you turn them on (both are off until you do; see section 4).

2. How it's used

Only to provide PeakSense's features to you:

  • finding highlight moments in your streams and cutting, captioning and reframing clips, montages and recaps;
  • showing your recent streams and matching your local recordings to your uploads;
  • posting to the accounts you connected, as described in the Terms: when you click Post now, or at the times you set in your posting queue;
  • the YouTube extras you allow or turn on: adding posts to playlists, caption tracks, title tests, a link to the full stream on a new Short, and the comment replies you write (see section 7);
  • showing how your posts do (Insights), which kinds of moments get watched most on your channel, so future picks lean toward them, and how long a recap should be;
  • media kits you choose to make on the Brand page (a picture and a page with your channel's numbers, made on your PC, for you to share with sponsors);
  • looking up which game you streamed from your stream title, tags or description;
  • sending notifications to your own Discord or Slack webhook, if you set one up.

We don't use your data for advertising, profiling or training AI models, and we never see it: it stays on your PC.

3. Where it's stored and how long it's kept

Everything is stored on your computer, not by us:

  • PeakSense's data folders, %LOCALAPPDATA%\PeakSense and %APPDATA%\PeakSense: the app's database, settings, sign-in tokens, logs, and the browser storage of the app's own window, where it remembers view preferences such as layouts and folded sections;
  • the folders you choose for imports and the video library, your OBS recording folder, and PeakSense's clip folders;
  • short-lived temporary files in Windows' temp folder, deleted when a job finishes.

The app doesn't use cookies to track you. Its window keeps only the view preferences above.

Retention:

  • Full-length videos PeakSense created or copied, such as OBS recordings it made and Twitch downloads, are moved to the Recycle Bin after the number of days you set (30 by default), or when your library goes over the size limit you set. You can pin videos to keep them, or turn the limits off. Your own imported files are only touched if you allow it.
  • YouTube data: statistics of your posts are refreshed within 30 days, or deleted if they can't be refreshed; the hourly view readings PeakSense takes in a post's first three days (for "best time to post") are deleted after 30 days; comments waiting for a reply are read again every few hours and only the latest unanswered ones are kept. See section 7 for the rest.
  • Media kits you make are files on your PC showing your numbers on the day you made them (the date is on the kit). They stay until you delete them on the Brand page.
  • Sign-in tokens are deleted and revoked with the platform when you disconnect that platform in PeakSense.
  • Everything else stays until you delete it or remove PeakSense's folders (see Data deletion).

Security: sign-in tokens, your OBS password and your licence key are stored encrypted with Windows' own data protection (DPAPI), which only your Windows account on your PC can unlock. The rest is protected by your Windows account, the same as your other files. Keep your PC and Windows account secure, and disconnect platforms you no longer use.

5. Live sessions (OBS and Twitch)

If you connect OBS, PeakSense watches your stream while you're live. It reads the recording OBS is writing on your PC, finds moments, and saves the recording to its local library when you stop.

With Twitch connected, it also records the time of chat messages and events. This lets it find chat spikes and lets you mark moments with a chat command. That log, including viewers' usernames and message text, is stored only on your PC next to the recording. You can delete it with the recording.

Nothing from a live session is sent to us.

6. Downloads from platforms

PeakSense never downloads videos from YouTube. For a stream you broadcast on YouTube, PeakSense uses your OBS recording, or a file you downloaded yourself from YouTube Studio and imported.

From Twitch, PeakSense downloads only VODs and clips of your own connected channel: it confirms through the Twitch API that the video belongs to the account you connected before downloading. Kick VOD links you paste are the one exception, because Kick provides no way to check; the Terms require them to be your own. You can turn platform downloads off in Settings → Storage and use OBS recordings or imported files only.

Downloads are saved on your PC and follow the storage rules in section 3.

What happens to YouTube data

  • Used for: the features in the table above only.
  • Stored: only on your PC, with sign-in tokens encrypted. Deleting PeakSense's data never deletes anything on YouTube: your videos, playlists, captions and comments there stay as they are.
  • Revoking access: disconnect YouTube in PeakSense, or remove PeakSense at Google's security settings page: https://security.google.com/settings/security/permissions.

PeakSense's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

8. TikTok

PeakSense uses TikTok's Login Kit and Content Posting API to post your clips to the TikTok account you connect. It asks for these permissions:

  • user.info.basic: your creator nickname and avatar, shown so you can see which account you're posting to;
  • video.publish: posting a clip directly when you click Post now in the editor, with the privacy and interaction settings you choose (nothing is preselected), after you confirm;
  • video.upload: sending scheduled clips to your TikTok inbox as drafts, which you finish and post in TikTok yourself;
  • video.list, once TikTok allows it for PeakSense: reading the view, like and comment counts of posts you made through PeakSense, for Insights. Until then PeakSense doesn't ask for it.
  • Before each post, PeakSense reads your posting options from TikTok (who can view, whether comments, Duets and Stitches are allowed, the longest video you can post) and shows them for you to choose.
  • Clips sent to TikTok go without PeakSense's watermark, and captions without the "Made with PeakSense" line.
  • TikTok data is stored only on your PC. Disconnecting TikTok in PeakSense revokes the token and deletes what PeakSense got from TikTok (post statistics and post IDs), at once.
  • You can also remove PeakSense in TikTok: Settings and privacy → Security → Manage app permissions.

See TikTok's Privacy Policy.

9. Instagram and Facebook

PeakSense can post your clips as Reels to Instagram and, if you choose, to a Facebook Page you manage. They are connected separately, and you don't need Facebook to use Instagram.

  • Instagram uses Instagram Login with the instagram_business_basic and instagram_business_content_publish permissions. PeakSense reads your username and account type (Instagram only allows posting to Creator or Business accounts) and publishes the Reels you choose, with your caption.
  • Facebook uses Facebook Login with the pages_show_list, pages_read_engagement and pages_manage_posts permissions. PeakSense reads your name and the list of Pages you manage so you can pick one, and publishes the Reels you choose to that Page.
  • What PeakSense doesn't do: it doesn't read your messages, followers or other posts, and it doesn't post anything you didn't choose or schedule.
  • Posting: posts from the editor go out when you click Post now. Scheduled posts (PeakSense Pro) go out at the times you set, and you can cancel them before then.
  • Storage: tokens, your username and Page names are stored only on your PC, with tokens encrypted. Instagram tokens last 60 days and are renewed automatically while you use PeakSense. We don't receive or keep any of it.
  • Disconnecting in PeakSense deletes the tokens from your PC. For Facebook it also removes PeakSense's access at Facebook. For Instagram, remove PeakSense in Instagram too: Settings → Website permissions → Apps and websites.

See the Instagram Privacy Policy and Meta Privacy Policy.

10. The website (peaksense.net)

peaksense.net is a static site with no ads and no analytics. If you join the launch list, your email address is collected by our list provider, LaunchList, which also loads a small script on the home page under its own privacy policy. We use the address only to email you once when PeakSense launches.

If you email us, we use your message only to reply. Our web host receives standard request information, such as your IP address and browser type, to serve the pages. We don't use analytics or advertising cookies.

To be removed from the list or have your emails deleted, write to support@peaksense.net. We act within 7 days.

11. Your choices and rights

  • Disconnect a platform in PeakSense (Settings → Connections). This revokes and deletes its token, and for YouTube and TikTok deletes what PeakSense got from them.
  • Remove one YouTube extra (Title & playlist edits, or Watch time) with Remove on the YouTube card, and keep the rest.
  • Revoke access at the platform:
    • Google: security settings
    • Twitch: Settings → Connections
    • TikTok: Settings and privacy → Security → Manage app permissions
    • Instagram: Settings → Website permissions → Apps and websites
    • Facebook: Settings & privacy → Settings → Apps and websites (or Business integrations for Pages)
  • Delete your data: follow Data deletion. Because the app's data lives only on your PC, deleting it there deletes it completely.
  • Access, correction and deletion requests for anything we hold (the relay items in section 4, launch-list email, support emails): write to support@peaksense.net. We delete within 7 days. Depending on where you live (for example under GDPR or US state privacy laws) you may have further rights, including to complain to your data-protection authority. We honour them.

12. Children

PeakSense is not directed at children. You must be at least 13 years old, and old enough to use each platform you connect under its own rules, to use PeakSense. We don't knowingly collect data from children. If you believe a child sent us their email, contact us and we'll delete it.
