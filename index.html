Where the AI comes from
HuggingFace

The AI behind the curtain.

HuggingFace hosts the open-source AI models PeakSense uses. The one that matters here is pyannote/speaker-diarization-3.1. Speaker diarization is the AI task of answering "who spoke when" — it splits your stream's audio into segments and tags each one with the person talking.

  • What diarization does — splits your audio into segments and labels each one with who's speaking, so a transcript reads like a conversation instead of a wall of text.
  • How PeakSense uses it — it runs after transcription and tags every line with a speaker, powering "Know who's talking": find a guest's best reaction, or pull the back-and-forths that made the stream.
  • Downloads pyannote/speaker-diarization-3.1 — the leading open model for speaker separation, running locally on your PC.
  • Completely optional — it's only needed for speaker separation on co-streams and podcasts. Captions, detection, and face finding all work without it.

Common questions

Things people ask before they try it

How much does PeakSense cost?

The free version has every tool — highlight detection, facecam layouts, captions, montages, playlist processing — used one clip at a time, with no caps and no watermark. PeakSense Pro is a one-time $69 that adds everything that runs without a click: new streams processed automatically, scheduled auto-posting, live auto-capture on Twitch, the automatic second look, scheduled Weekly Peak reels, bulk tools, analytics, and your logo on every export. No subscription, no per-clip charges.

Does PeakSense upload my videos anywhere?

No. Downloading, detection, clipping, and captioning all run on your computer. PeakSense only goes online to pull a stream you asked for, check your channels for new streams, or post a clip to your own account.

What kind of computer do I need?

A Windows 10 or 11 PC with about 20 GB of free space. An NVIDIA GPU makes it fast. AMD and CPU-only PCs work too — AI transcription runs on the processor, so it takes longer; pick a smaller transcription model in Settings if speed matters more than accuracy.

Do I need to install anything else?

PeakSense works on its own. Two optional, free extras unlock more: Ollama, for AI scoring of your moments, and a Hugging Face account, for labeling who's talking on co-streams. Posting to YouTube needs a one-time Google setup that PeakSense walks you through.

How does the license work?

You get a key that starts with PS-. Paste it into Settings and you're done. It never expires, there's no subscription, and PeakSense checks it offline — no calls to our servers.

Is the free version a real product or a trial?

It's the real product, with no time limit. Every tool works — PeakSense even tells you when a new stream is ready; you just click Process and post clips yourself. Pro is for people who want it to happen on its own.

Will it clip the same moment twice?

No. PeakSense remembers what it has already clipped from each stream and skips those moments when you process it again, then fills your clip count with new ones.

Is there a limit on YouTube uploads?

Not from us. YouTube itself limits how many videos an app can upload per day for each account's Google setup, so heavy posters may hit YouTube's daily cap. PeakSense shows how much you have left.

What's the story with TikTok posting?

TikTok's Content Posting API restricts apps that haven't completed an audit yet. Until PeakSense finishes that audit, TikTok posts land as private (self-only) rather than going public. The dashboard says so plainly.

How is this different from other clip tools?

Three things. It runs on your computer, so your footage never gets uploaded. There are no credits — process as many streams as your hardware can handle. And it's built for streamers: chat spikes, facecam layouts, Twitch live capture, and a watcher that picks up your new VODs automatically.
